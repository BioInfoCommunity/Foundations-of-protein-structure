Principles of protein folding and architecture

In the previous section, we introduced amino acids and the geometric constraints of the peptide backbone. We also established that the primary structure (i.e. the amino acid sequence) contains the information needed for a protein to adopt a characteristic three-dimensional shape under the right conditions.

Here, we build the next layers of understanding: how regular backbone hydrogen-bonding patterns generate secondary structure, and how those elements pack together into a stable tertiary structure. Along the way, we will connect these structural levels to the physical forces that stabilise proteins, and introduce the modern “energy landscape” view of folding.

By the end of this section, you will be able to:

  • Identify α-helices and β-sheets in a protein structure and describe their hydrogen-bonding patterns.
  • Differentiate between structural motifs, domains, and folds.
  • Analyse tertiary structure through domains and structural classification.
  • Relate protein stability and folding to biological function, including the consequences of misfolding and aggregation.
