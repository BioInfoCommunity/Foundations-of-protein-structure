# Summary

Proteins are the functional products of genetic information. The central dogma describes the directional flow from DNA to RNA to protein, but biological activity ultimately depends on how a polypeptide chain folds into a defined three-dimensional structure.

The genetic code translates nucleotide information into an amino acid sequence. Its redundancy provides robustness, while codon usage bias influences expression efficiency across organisms. Although many mutations are tolerated, some substitutions alter chemical properties in ways that significantly impact structure and function.

All proteins are built from 20 standard amino acids. Each shares a common backbone architecture but differs in its side chain chemistry.

Amino acids are joined by peptide bonds, forming a directional polypeptide chain (N → C). While the peptide bond itself is planar and rigid, rotation around adjacent bonds generates torsion angles (φ and ψ) that define the backbone conformation. Only certain combinations of these angles are sterically allowed, as visualised in the Ramachandran plot.

The primary structure,  the amino acid sequence, encodes all the information required for folding. Sequence determines chemical properties, constrains conformational space, and ultimately enables function. Structural reasoning, therefore, begins with sequence analysis.
