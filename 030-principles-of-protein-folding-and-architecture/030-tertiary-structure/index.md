# Tertiary structure

Having explored the secondary structure elements of a protein, α-helices and β-sheets and the elements that connect them, we now turn to the next level of structural organisation: tertiary structure. Tertiary structure describes the complete three-dimensional shape adopted by a single polypeptide chain when its secondary-structure elements pack together. While the folding process could, in theory, generate an infinite number of shapes, evolution has converged on a limited set of recurring architectural solutions.

The overall fold is essential for defining a protein’s biochemical function, stability and ability to interact with other protein partners. The tertiary structure is stabilised by weak interactions involving both polar and nonpolar groups.
