# Protein dynamics, interactions, and modifications

Previously, we explored how individual proteins fold into stable three-dimensional structures composed of domains built from secondary structural elements. However, understanding the structure of an isolated protein is only the beginning. Biological function emerges not from static structures but from dynamic interactions—proteins binding to other proteins, recognising specific DNA sequences, catalysing chemical reactions on small molecule substrates, and undergoing conformational changes in response to cellular signals.

This section explores how proteins function in their native context: the crowded, dynamic environment of the cell. We will examine quaternary structure, the assembly of multiple polypeptide chains into functional complexes.

By the end of this section, you will be able to:

* Explain the principle of molecular complementarity in protein–protein and protein–ligand interactions.
* Define quaternary structure and distinguish it from tertiary structure.
* Distinguish between affinity and specificity in molecular binding.
* Describe the functional importance of protein flexibility across different timescales.
